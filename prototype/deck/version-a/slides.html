What is a PDE?

A partial differential equation (PDE) is an equation that involves an unknown function of several independent variables and its partial derivatives with respect to those variables.

F(x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ, u, ∂u/∂x₁, ∂u/∂x₂, ..., ∂²u/∂x₁∂x₂, ...) = 0
  • u = unknown function (dependent variable)
  • x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ = independent variables
  • Partial derivatives = derivatives with respect to one variable, holding others fixed
3

Why PDEs Matter

PDEs are the mathematical language for describing how things change in space and time.

  • Predicting weather and climate
  • Designing aircraft and automobiles
  • Modeling heat transfer in buildings
  • Simulating electrical signals in the brain
  • Understanding financial markets (Black-Scholes)
  • Modeling population dynamics
4

The Three Classical PDEs

Heat Equation (Parabolic)

∂u/∂t = α ∇²u

Describes temperature diffusion over time.

Wave Equation (Hyperbolic)

∂²u/∂t² = c² ∇²u

Describes wave propagation.

Laplace Equation (Elliptic)

∇²u = 0

Describes steady-state equilibrium.

Classification of PDEs

For a second-order linear PDE in two variables:

A ∂²u/∂x² + 2B ∂²u/∂x∂y + C ∂²u/∂y² + ... = 0

Classification depends on the discriminant B² - AC:

ConditionTypeExample
B² - AC < 0EllipticLaplace equation
B² - AC = 0ParabolicHeat equation
B² - AC > 0HyperbolicWave equation

Boundary and Initial Conditions

A PDE alone does not determine a unique solution. You need additional conditions:

  • Initial conditions: u(x, 0) = f(x) — the initial state
  • Boundary conditions: What happens at the edges of the domain

Types of Boundary Conditions

  • Dirichlet: u is specified on the boundary
  • Neumann: ∂u/∂n is specified on the boundary
  • Robin: A combination of u and ∂u/∂n
7

Solution Methods

Separation of Variables

Assume u(x,t) = X(x)·T(t). Substitute into the PDE to get two ODEs.

u(x,t) = Σ Bₙ sin(nπx/L) e^{-α(nπ/L)²t}

Numerical Methods

  • Finite Difference Methods (FDM): Replace derivatives with differences on a grid
  • Finite Element Methods (FEM): Approximate using piecewise polynomials
  • Finite Volume Methods (FVM): Conservation laws on control volumes
8

Summary

  • PDEs involve unknown functions of multiple variables and their partial derivatives
  • Three classical types: Heat (parabolic), Wave (hyperbolic), Laplace (elliptic)
  • Classification determines solution methods and physical behavior
  • Boundary and initial conditions are required for unique solutions
  • Solution methods include separation of variables, Fourier analysis, and numerical methods
  • PDEs are essential for modeling real-world phenomena
